Experimental Assistant Platform

An assistant that can stay useful.

We are testing how an assistant can combine speech, visual evidence, confirmed memory and device interaction without pretending uncertainty is knowledge.

HearSpeech input with review and correction.
SeeReason over current visual evidence instead of substituting an older image.
RememberCarry confirmed facts and corrections forward with evidence boundaries.
Act carefullyPrepare useful actions while keeping consequential external actions behind approval.
Stable identity + learned relationship + contextual presentation. The goal is not to make an assistant imitate its user; it is to let it learn how to be itself with that user.

Experimental platform. Capabilities are tested incrementally; future capabilities are not represented as finished products. Internal architecture, prompts and implementation details remain private.

For business owners

What annoying thing do you do over and over?

That is often where useful automation starts. We like the small, weird problems that do not justify buying another giant software suite: a repetitive handoff, a forgotten follow-up, a status board, a field note, a customer-facing display, or a workflow that should have been one button.

The first question is not “what app can we sell you?” It is “what should stop wasting your time?”

About

Build the missing piece.

Free Frequency is a small experimental technology workshop led by Shannon Bishop. The work spans practical browser tools, sensing, automation, unusual-experience research and assistant systems.

Free is there for more than one reason: free to use when that makes sense, open to experiment, free to repurpose useful hardware and ideas, and free to follow a question far enough to find out what is actually happening.
The human behind the squirrels
Shannon Bishop

I’m a builder and systems thinker from Midland, Michigan. My goal is to wake up more open-minded than I was yesterday—and willing to revise what I think I know.

I notice patterns and pull threads that others might overlook. I follow interesting questions wherever they lead: software, hardware, sound, strange ideas, practical problems and the occasional rabbit hole.

The goal isn’t certainty. It isn’t to find miracles. It’s to find mechanisms.
